## What this domain is

The population is larger and differently named than the brief assumed: there are 48 gateway controller classes in app/Http/Controllers, of which only 20 are spelled *PaymentController.php (five more use the upstream typo *Paymnet*Controller, and 23 are plain *Controller), plus PaymentCallbackController (a Paytm server-to-server callback, gated off) and Paymera, which has no controller of its own and lives inside PaymentController. Every one of these classes serves two unrelated arms: a SaaS PLAN purchase (Phase 06) and a storefront ORDER payment (Phase 04), and the controls on the two arms are completely different. The plan arm is comprehensively hardened and pinned — 26 forgeable providers are fail-closed behind config/payments.php `plan_activation` (default false), 18 are recorded as provider-verified, BankTransfer is operator-approved, and five test files assert the classification in both directions. The storefront arm is protected by one central control rather than per-gateway ones: PaymentVerificationService::resolveOrderPaymentStatus (app/Services/PaymentVerificationService.php:339-350) writes Pending for any gateway payment with no verification record, and only five code paths produce such a record (Paymera, AuthorizeNet, Khalti, NMI, PaiementPro), so 43 of 48 providers write Pending orders by deliberate owner decision; all seven storefront order writers take a claimGatewayReturn() replay claim, and amounts are compared only for paymera/nmi/khalti. The single most important thing a reader must know is that this central control is the ONLY thing standing between a forged storefront return and a falsely-paid order, because almost no storefront return verifies anything itself — and the one gateway that does make the outbound call, CyberSource, throws the answer away (CyberSourceController.php:364-365) and is additionally broken on three further independent counts that no test touches.
