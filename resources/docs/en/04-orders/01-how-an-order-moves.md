## The shape of it

A customer places an order. Stock comes down. You see it in the dashboard and in the admin app.
You move it through its statuses as the real world catches up: confirmed, prepared, sent,
delivered.

The status is not decoration. It is what the customer is told, so a status you set because it
looked tidy is a message you sent.

## Telling the customer

Status changes notify the customer. Change the status when the thing has happened, not when you
intend it to happen today. "Sent" on a parcel still in the shop is the single fastest way to turn a
patient customer into a worried one.

## Finding an order

Search by order number, by customer, or by date. The order number is what a customer will quote,
so ask for it first — it is unambiguous in a way that "the blue one from Tuesday" is not.

## Changing an order

What you can change depends on how far it has gone and on how it was paid. An order that has not
been paid is easier to amend than one that has. If in doubt, cancel and take a new order rather
than editing a paid one into something it was not.
